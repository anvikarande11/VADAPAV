The idea

Simple on the surface.
Deliberate underneath.

Heirloom is designed around a simple principle: the person who owns the information should define the rules for when, why and how it can be passed on.

01 / ORGANIZE

Put the important things in order.

Documents, digital accounts, assets, instructions and the context someone would actually need — organized into one intentional vault.

02 / DEFINE

Decide before the moment arrives.

Choose the people, conditions and thresholds that should govern a future handover instead of leaving others to guess.

03 / PROTECT

Make access harder to misuse.

Recovery is treated as a process, not a button: independent confirmation, a delay and an opportunity to intervene.

Why this model

Trust should be distributed, not assumed.

Heirloom's defining idea is not simply storing information securely. It is reducing the amount of trust any one person or platform needs to hold.

Built around failure

The safeguards matter
when life gets messy.

Good inheritance design has to handle uncertainty, unavailable people, false alarms and last-minute intervention — not just the happy path.

01 / NO SINGLE KEY

Distributed authorization

No one guardian should be able to unilaterally release protected information.

02 / TIME

A window to intervene

A legitimate owner should have a meaningful chance to cancel or stop a mistaken recovery attempt.

03 / AUDIT

A visible history

Recovery attempts, confirmations, cancellations and releases should leave a trace that can be reviewed.

The Heirloom model

Make the right thing easier to do.

Heirloom separates three ideas that are often mixed together: storing information, deciding who should receive it, and proving that the moment for handover has actually arrived.

The interface keeps those ideas visible without exposing implementation details. The result is meant to feel calm when everything is normal — and deliberate when something goes wrong.
